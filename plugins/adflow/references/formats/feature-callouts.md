# Feature callouts

The product shown large with lines or arrows running to labelled parts, each label naming a feature.

Family: Static, text-led.
Back to the [gallery](../format-gallery.md) · rules in [format-classification.md](../format-classification.md).

## Must be true

- One product is shown large, usually in the centre.
- Lines, arrows or dots connect labels to specific parts of it.
- Usually three to six labels; each only makes sense next to the part it points at.

## Not to confuse with

- **Headline + benefits:** benefits about the whole product, nothing points.
- **Diagram:** a chart that helps choose between options, not the parts of one product.
- **Product hero:** the packshot without labels.

## Quick test

Does each label point at a specific part of the product?

## Does not belong in the format

- "How it works" or the mechanism as the message: Angle.
- A 3D render or exploded product shot: production, a tag.

## Variants

- **Pointout:** the product in the middle with leader lines to labels around it. Tag it as Feature callouts.

## Examples

- ✅ A backpack with arrows to the hidden laptop sleeve, the waterproof zip and the padded straps.
- ✅ A face cream jar with lines to the pump ("no fingers in the cream") and the refill pod, for a hygiene angle.
- ❌ The backpack with three benefit ticks under a headline, no arrows: Headline + benefits.
- Real ads: the visual-formats library, files named `vf=feature-benefit-pointout` (6 examples), and its notes in

## When it works

General signal from the sources, not a verdict for a brand. For a brand, its own performance in Adflow decides:
spend first, then ROAS, per creative family.

Good for products with features you can see (gallery summary of Adcrate and the swipe library).
