# POV

The camera is the user's eyes: first-person footage with their hands in frame.

Family: Video, footage with voice or text.
Back to the [gallery](../format-gallery.md) · rules in [format-classification.md](../format-classification.md).

## Must be true

- The camera sits at the user's eye level and looks where they look.
- Their hands are in frame doing something with the product; their face is not seen.
- The first-person view lasts for most of the video.

## Not to confuse with

- **Product demo:** the same use filmed from the side or front.
- **Vlog:** the camera follows a person whose face you see, through their day.
- **Unboxing / Try-on:** when opening a package or wearing the product is the point, those cards win, even
  first-person.

## Quick test

Do you look through the user's eyes, with their hands in frame, for most of the video? Check what is on screen in the
per-second analysis in Adflow, not the thumbnail or the ad name.

## Does not belong in the format

- A "POV: you…" caption over another form: that is copy, not the camera angle.
- Head-mounted or phone camera, staged or real: production.

## Variants

- **Routine POV:** a daily moment, such as making coffee or packing a bag. Tag it as POV.

## Examples

- ✅ First-person hands make a morning matcha: scoop, whisk, pour, with soft music and a few lines of text.
- ✅ First-person hands pack a gym bag, each item clicking into its pocket while a voice names it.
- ❌ The same matcha made, filmed from across the counter: Product demo.
- Real ads: No examples in the library yet.

## When it works

General signal from the sources, not a verdict for a brand. For a brand, its own performance in Adflow decides:
spend first, then ROAS, per creative family.
