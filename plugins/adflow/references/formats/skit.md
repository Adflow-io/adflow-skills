# Skit

An acted scene with roles, where the exchange between characters carries the ad.

Family: Video, footage with voice or text.
Back to the [gallery](../format-gallery.md) · rules in [format-classification.md](../format-classification.md).

## Must be true

- Two or more characters are on screen; one person playing two roles counts.
- They act a scene with dialogue or action between them, with a setup and a payoff.

## Not to confuse with

- **Talking head:** one person speaking to camera as themselves.
- **Vlog:** real moments of a day, not a scripted scene with roles.
- **Phone call:** the call is the frame; an acted scene face to face is a Skit.

## Quick test

Is it a scripted scene between characters, and does their exchange carry the ad? Check who speaks and when in the
per-second analysis in Adflow, not the thumbnail or the ad name.

## Does not belong in the format

- Funny, relatable, "us vs them": tone and message, Angle.
- Actors, the founder playing a role, AI characters: Creator or production.

## Variants

- **Talking back and forth:** one person plays two roles, cut between them. Tag it as Skit.
- **Clone:** the same person appears twice in one shot. Tag it as Skit.

## Examples

- ✅ A man struggles to fold a fitted sheet; his partner walks in and shows him the sheet with labelled corners.
- ✅ One woman plays herself before and after her first cup of a mushroom coffee, cut back and forth.
- ❌ The same woman telling the camera how she feels before and after her coffee: Talking head.

General signal from the sources, not a verdict for a brand. For a brand, its own performance in Adflow decides:
spend first, then ROAS, per creative family.

The gallery (from its sources) rates it strong for relatable problems. Not a rule for every brand.
