# Judging an existing video ad

How to tell what happens in a video ad that already exists: its format, who is on screen, who speaks, and what the
hook is. Use it whenever a skill describes a winner, a reference or an existing ad, for example to replicate a winner,
pick a format, or tag a concept.

## Rules

1. **Judge from the per-second analysis in Adflow** (the segments of the asset): per second what is on screen, the
   text on screen, and what is said. Never judge a video from its thumbnail, a single frame or the ad name. A
   thumbnail shows one moment and the ad name says what was planned, not what was made.
2. **Speech is not song lyrics.** Check where the audio comes from. When it is marked as lyrics or sung, nobody is
   speaking: there is only music. A video with only music and text on screen is not a talking head or a voice-over.
3. **Text on screen is not always a caption.** Tell subtitles of what is said apart from text that carries the ad on
   its own, such as a comment bubble or a headline.
4. **Names are not in the analysis.** It describes people ("a woman in a kitchen"), not who they are. Do not conclude
   that a founder or a specific creator is on screen unless the brand or the user confirms it.
5. **No analysis, no verdict.** When the segments are missing or failed for a video, say so and leave it open, or ask
   the user to look. Do not fall back to the thumbnail.
6. **Many videos?** Judge the ones that carry the most spend first, and say how much of the spend you could not check.

Statics may be judged from the image, but read the text on them yourself: the automatic description can misread it.
