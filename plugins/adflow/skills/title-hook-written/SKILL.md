# Title Hook (written)

Delivers the **title card** of a video: the short text shown on screen in the first seconds, written to sit next to the spoken hook. It serves the video voorzet and script steps of the [ad pipeline](../../references/ad-pipeline.md), after the spoken hook exists or at the same time as it.

The two hooks have different jobs:
- **Spoken hook = right brain.** Feeling, identity, emotion, worldview. It makes the viewer feel something.
- **Title card = left brain.** Concrete, specific, clear, curiosity-driven. It makes the topic obvious and scroll-stopping.

So the title card never repeats the spoken hook. It makes the topic concrete where the spoken hook makes it felt.

In one second the title card must answer:
1. What is this video about?
2. Is this relevant to me?
3. Why should I care?

## Input (infer from the running context, only ask for what is really missing)
- **The video script.** Required. If it is not there, ask for it.
- **The spoken hook.** Usually the first lines of the script. If the user shares several hooks, run the steps below once per hook.
- **Slot and customer words** (persona × angle × awareness), when known: use the words the audience itself uses.
- **Language and house rules of the brand** (brand instructions or memory): follow those. Default to the language of the script.

## Steps
1. **Read the script** and find the most concrete, true detail: a result, a number, a time span, an object, a before/after, a surprising fact. That is the raw material.
2. **Note what the spoken hook already does** (feeling, identity, claim), so the title card adds something instead of echoing it.
3. **Write 10 title cards** that differ from each other: different concrete details, different sentence shapes (result, "this is what…", "I replaced X with Y", a number, a time span, a named object). No reworded twins.
4. **Check each one** against the rules below and cut or rewrite what fails.
5. **Pick the best 3**, then **one #1 recommendation**, and explain in two or three lines why that one works.
6. **Add an emoji** only where it fits naturally and adds meaning. Most title cards need none.

## Rules
1. **3–9 words.** Readable in one second.
2. **Simple, spoken language.** The way a person would say it to a friend. In Dutch it must sound native, not like a translation from English.
3. **Concrete over clever.** A number, a time span, an object or a result beats a metaphor. If the line could belong to any video, it is too vague.
4. **Curiosity from what is left out, not from vagueness.** The viewer should wonder what happens next because the topic is clear but the outcome is not.
5. **It must match the video.** The script has to deliver what the title card promises. No clickbait.
6. **Nothing invented.** Numbers, results, time spans and claims come from the script or from what the brand can back up. If the script has no number, don't add one. Mind the brand's rules on health, income and before/after claims.
7. **No word-for-word repeat of the spoken hook.** Same topic is fine, same sentence is not.
8. **Avoid:** vague motivational lines, poetic wording, corporate language, long sentences.

## Patterns that work (invented examples, only to show the shape)
- Result + time span: "Zo verdween mijn rugpijn in 3 weken"
- Replacement: "I replaced my morning coffee with this"
- Cost/effort contrast: "Gezond ontbijt voor minder dan €2 per dag"
- Hidden problem: "This is what's hiding in your cutting board"
- Long-term mistake: "Ik kookte 10 jaar met de verkeerde pannen"
- Small change, quick proof: "My dog stopped scratching after 1 week"

Use these to see what "concrete and specific" looks like, never to copy a template: the detail must come from this script.

## Output
For each hook, in this order:

1. **10 title card options** (numbered)
2. **Best 3** (pick from the 10)
3. **#1 recommendation**
4. **Why it works** (two or three lines, in plain words)

Emoji suggestions go next to an option only where they fit. No other intro or summary. With several hooks, repeat the block per hook under a short heading.

Next the user picks a title card (or asks for another round) and puts it in the concept document or the editor brief.

## Notes
- Fits next to `power-hooks` (flow P), `hook-levers` (flow L) and `gap-hook`: those write the spoken hook, this writes the text on screen. For static headlines use `static-voorzet`.
- Keep it client-agnostic: no client examples in this file.
