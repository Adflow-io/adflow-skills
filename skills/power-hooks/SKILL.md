# Power Hooks

A hook lives or dies on a few building blocks, not on clever wording. This skill builds and
sharpens hooks by stacking **6 power words** (chunks, really — they can be words or short phrases).
The first four are the engine; the last two are boosters you add only when they earn their place.

> **Flow P.** This is one of two separate hook flows. The other is **flow L** (`hook-levers`: the lever sets the form,
> its example is the sentence template). Keep them apart for hook diversity. **Don't score flow-L hooks with Mode C** —
> levers like naming, condensing, symbol, paradox or question hold the claim back on purpose.

## The 6 power words

1. **Subject** — who/what the hook is about (*you, je kind, we, this AI tool*). The viewer drops
   themselves into the subject's seat, so make it someone they identify with.
2. **Action** — the action the subject takes to reach the result (*grow, verbinden, wash, doe dit*).
3. **Objective / end-state** — the result the action produces. **The more desirable or surprising
   the result, the stronger the hook.** This is the payoff the viewer is buying into.
4. **Contrast** — the jump from base state → new state (A→B). It manufactures the curiosity gap
   ("how do you get from A to B?"). Often the *same* phrase does double duty as objective **and**
   contrast — that's a sign of tight writing (*"0 → 100K subs"*, *"in maar 5 minuten"*).
5. **Proof** *(optional)* — why the viewer should believe you (*"again", "al 30 jaar", a number,
   a result*). Matters most for educational/claim-based content; rarely needed for pure entertainment.
6. **Time** *(optional)* — a time or speed component that adds urgency/desirability
   (*"in 5 minuten", "in the next 90 days"*). Speed-to-result is inherently magnetic.

**Two things that keep hooks tight:** (a) proof and time don't have to sit in the hook itself —
they can land in line 2–3. (b) The more *concrete and specific* the objective and contrast, the
harder the hook hits. Vague results make weak hooks.

**What to avoid:** generic "bold claim / shocking statement" openers that everyone uses — when every
ad opens the same way, it stops reading as bold and starts reading as *"this is an ad trying to sell
me something."* Specificity and a real contrast beat volume.

## Three modes

Figure out which one the user needs (often obvious from their ask), then follow that mode.

### Mode A — Generate hooks
1. Nail down the raw materials first: the **subject** (who it's for), the **objective/end-state**
   (the result the product/insight delivers), and the **contrast** (what's the frustrating base
   state it replaces?). Pull these from the offer, the audience's own words, or reviews if available.
2. Stack the 4 core words into a line; layer in **proof** and/or **time** only where they sharpen it.
3. Give **a few strong suggestions — 3, at most 4. Not a long list.** Make them genuinely different
   in which lever leads (e.g. one contrast-forward, one time-forward, one objective-forward) so each
   is a real alternative, not a reworded twin. Fewer, sharper options respect the user's attention.
4. **Every suggestion gets a one-line "why"** — the reason it works (which power word/lever it leans on
   and the effect), not just a list of tags. The why is the point; that's what the user is asking for.

### Mode B — Strengthen an existing hook
1. Break the hook down against the 6 words and name what's **missing** (usually a fuzzy objective,
   no contrast, or no time).
2. Rewrite it 2–3 ways, adding the highest-leverage missing pieces. Keep the user's voice and the
   audience's language — don't sand it into generic marketing-speak.
3. For each rewrite, add a one-line *why it's stronger* (which power word you added).

### Mode C — Score a hook
Rate 0–6: +1 for each power word clearly present (the 4 core ones matter most). Call out what's
missing and what to add. A hook missing 2+ of the core four is weak regardless of polish.

## Output format

Lean and scannable. **Just the hooks and a short why — no intro, no summary, no filler.** Each
suggestion = the hook + one line explaining why it works. Cap the why at a single line.

```
1. <hook>
   waarom: <one line>
2. <hook>
   waarom: <one line>
3. <hook>
   waarom: <one line>
```

For a strengthen pass, show **Was → Nu** with a one-line reason (max 3 rewrites):

```
Was: Verbinden met je kind hoeft niet groots te zijn
Nu:  Echt verbinden met je kind — in maar 5 minuten per dag
waarom: tijd toegevoegd → doet dubbel werk als contrast (groot resultaat, kleine moeite)
```

## Worked examples

**Generate (product: family yoga cards, audience: busy parents):**
- Echt verbinden met je kind — in maar 5 minuten per dag  `[action · subject · time+contrast]`
- In 5 minuten van gehaast naar écht samen  `[contrast · time]`
- Denk je dat verbinden tijd kost? 5 minuten is genoeg  `[belief-flip · objective · time]`

**Strengthen:**
- Was: *"This ad tanks."* `[subject · action]` → Nu: *"I made the perfect ad — and it got beat by a
  text-only one made in 2 minutes."* `[subject · action · objective · contrast · time]`

## Notes

- **Scope:** this skill is for **video** hooks/openers. Static-ad headlines follow their own playbook
  (the headline is the ad; "assume no one knows or cares"; clarity↔curiosity) — see
  `references/static-formats.md` and `references/designer-brief-static.md`, not this skill.

- Match the hook's language to the audience — reuse their exact phrasing (from reviews/comments) when
  you have it; borrowed words feel native and stop the scroll better than polished copy.
- To find proven hooks in a niche, best-performing-video tools (e.g. sandcastles.ai) let you sort a
  creator's posts by views/engagement and reverse-engineer the power-word stack.
- Fuller framework notes and client-specific winning-hook examples may live in the user's project
  (e.g. an `references/hook-frameworks.md`); consult it when working within such a project.
